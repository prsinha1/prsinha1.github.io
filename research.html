Research

Methods for learning in dynamic environments

My research studies learning systems in which data distributions, representations, and patterns of collaboration change over time. I develop methods that use feedback and structural information to preserve useful variation, support stable learning, and adapt decisions under incomplete observations.

Current theme

Learning under feedback and evolving distributions

How can learning systems use their own performance signals to change who collaborates, where information flows, and which models receive data?

Ongoing research

Stable Collaboration in Heterogeneous Federated Learning

In heterogeneous federated learning, a single global model may not serve all clients equally well. I develop methods that use client performance feedback to infer compatibility, form stable collaboration groups, and adapt aggregation weights through no regret learning. The objective is to promote information sharing among compatible clients while reducing harmful aggregation across incompatible distributions.

Related published work

Accepted workshop paper, DynaFront 2 at NeurIPS 2026

Collapse and Recovery in Clustered Federated Learning

I study initialization induced cluster collapse by treating cluster models as the state of a controlled dynamical system and client routing as the control input. In a two group linear model, I characterize a starvation basin and derive finite horizon recovery conditions based on persistent selective exposure. Client side blocked EXP3 controllers recover distinct specialists in linear and Gaussian mixture settings, while transformed FEMNIST experiments identify a coordination limit for slower neural specialization.

02

Current theme

Generative models and longitudinal representations

What structure do generative models preserve, and can their internal representations separate meaningful temporal change from persistent individual variation?

Ongoing research

Longitudinal Representation Learning with Diffusion Models

I study whether pretrained diffusion models encode structured temporal change in a form that can be separated from subject specific variation. Differences between baseline and follow up diffusion activations are analyzed using Recursive Feature Machines and Average Gradient Outer Product geometry. The objective is to extract low dimensional change coordinates for sequential monitoring with empirically calibrated false alarm control.

Accepted poster abstract, NYSDS 2026

Rare Event Fidelity in Diffusion Models

I study when rare or low probability structure is preserved or lost during diffusion learning and reverse sampling. The objective is to identify and improve failures in representing parts of a distribution that occur infrequently but remain important.

03

Current theme

Adaptive data selection and distributed optimization

How should a learning system select data and coordinate across a network when the useful information changes across time, nodes, and model states?

Preprint in preparation

Distributed Dynamic Inverse Problems

I develop a decentralized Consensus ADMM method for networks whose nodes have heterogeneous observability. Edge weights and coordinate wise masks encode which information is reliable at each node, so consensus is enforced only on coordinates for which nodes possess useful information. I use multiplicative weight updates to adapt these masks with regret guarantees, producing communication patterns that approach the best fixed consensus structure in hindsight.

Manuscript in preparation

Dynamic Coreset Selection for Large Scale Machine Learning

I develop a dynamic coreset selection method that adapts the selected data subset as the model evolves during training. The method uses multilinear extension and continuous greedy optimization to preserve gradient accuracy while substantially reducing the amount of data used.
